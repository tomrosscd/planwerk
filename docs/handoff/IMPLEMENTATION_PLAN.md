## University design and flow

Visual direction: dark institutional prestige with mint university seal drawn in SVG, huge confident headline and tiny administrative metadata. A diploma itself should contrast: warm cream paper, ink typography, mint seal, fine rules, substantial margins.

Structure:
1. Eyebrow: PLANWERK UNIVERSITY / EST. PRE-LAUNCH.
2. Headline: Prestige. Without prerequisites.
3. Sub: A world-class education in knowing whether people are busy. No prior knowledge, software access or demonstrated aptitude required.
4. CTA: Begin your qualification (anchor to exam).
5. Three deadpan metrics: 100% pass rate; 01 exam question; 0 external accrediting bodies.
6. Three syllabus blocks: Introduction to Availability; Applied Calendar Looking; Advanced Framework Confidence. Course links, if shown, should really take the visitor to the exam. No dead buttons.
7. Exam form: name field (required, 70-ish character limit), one required radio question: "Is the team busy?" Options: Yes; No; We need a meeting to establish that. All answers pass, each with a different congratulatory observation.
8. On submit: prevent network submission, use DOM textContent for user data, show pass state, accessible live announcement, personalised certificate preview, download actions and badges.
9. Allow correcting the name/retaking without reloading. A certificate date and unique-ish credential ID should remain stable through downloads for the same completion.
10. Do not store names in localStorage or send them anywhere. Say "Your name stays in this browser."

Certificate:
- "PlanWerk University"
- "Certificate of Questionable Distinction"
- recipient name, prominent and fitted to available width
- "Certified Framework Practitioner"
- "Having successfully answered one question, and demonstrated sufficient confidence in the answer."
- issue date, credential number e.g. PWU-2026-ABC123
- Sebastian Klett, Founder & CEO; Tom Ross, Head of Gemini
- joke footnote: "Recognised by PlanWerk. Recognition elsewhere remains a matter for elsewhere."
- clearly humorous and not a real accreditation claim

Download implementation:
- Use local pdf-lib browser bundle; copy licence with bundle.
- Draw a high-resolution certificate on HTML canvas (e.g. 2526x1785 landscape, A4 ratio), including original logo/seal and text. Use Canvas text metrics to shrink/wrap long names; support Unicode with browser fonts.
- Show canvas or equivalent image preview with accessible text alternative.
- Download a real PDF generated by pdf-lib embedding the high-resolution canvas PNG on a landscape A4 page. This supports arbitrary names without WinAnsi/font embedding failures. A high-resolution image PDF is an acceptable tradeoff here.
- Optionally offer certificate PNG as well, but PDF is required.
- Use Blob/object URL download with delayed revoke, human-readable sanitised filenames.
- Handle download errors visibly; reset disabled/loading state in finally.
- Generate/offer dark and light badge PNGs and SVGs. PNGs are suitable for email signatures. These should say "PlanWerk Certified / Framework Practitioner" and match original logo.
- Do NOT make users download executable files or sign up.

## Competitor comparison

Visual direction: dark SaaS comparison page, large declarative heading, readable table, honest footnotes delivered dryly.

Hero: "A category of one. If you define it carefully."
Sub: "We compared PlanWerk with the tools teams use today. The methodology was developed by PlanWerk. The results were encouraging."

Comparison columns:
- PlanWerk (mint highlighted)
- Excel
- A whiteboard
- Asking someone
- A rough idea

Rows should be funny AND acknowledge common-sense competitors' strengths. Use COPY_AND_CONSTRAINTS.md. Keep factual comparisons general, avoiding brittle claims about actual product features/prices.

On mobile, allow horizontal scrolling of the table with an explicit swipe hint and a labelled keyboard-focusable scroll region. Never squeeze six columns into 375px or cause body overflow. Prefer sticky first column if layering/readability is verified.

Follow-up sections:
- Three "Why PlanWerk" statements: Proprietary terminology; Global corporate ambition; A founder who will explain it again.
- Methodology note: "Conducted internally. Reviewed internally. Celebrated internally."
- CTA to University: "Become qualified to agree with us."

No actual product purchase, competitor logos, fake reviewer attribution or fabricated quantitative performance evidence.

## Open letter

Visual direction: warm cream, near-black ink, original newspaper-style masthead, enormous serif headline, thin/double rules, multi-column body with editorial section headings, oversized pull quote, signed closing. Use a serif Google Font such as Source Serif 4/Playfair Display, with sensible local serif fallback. Do not copy NYT masthead/trademarks or claim NYT publication.

Suggested masthead: THE PLANWERK LETTER.
Meta strip: VOL. 01 / PUBLIC STATEMENT / A MATTER OF PRINCIPLE AND DNS.
Headline: "We know who you are."
Deck: "An open letter to the independent third party currently holding planwerk.com.au, and to a market that deserves better."

Write a substantial, coherent 900-1200-word letter. Tone: CEO hostile-takeover open letter, solemn fiduciary language applied to forgetting to buy a domain. Escalate absurdity, not real threats.

Required beats:
- Speak directly to anonymous domain holder.
- Identity withheld owing to ongoing proceedings in the fictional Court of Deeply Held Feelings. Do not assert actual litigation or criminal wrongdoing.
- Admit the other party bought the domain first using ordinary available means. The board considers this technically permissible and spiritually unacceptable.
- Distinguish legal ownership from the company's enormous sense of entitlement.
- Real product has not launched; domain viewed as existential matter despite .life working perfectly.
- "We will take it back" phrased as an absurd determination to make a politely worded offer, not hacking, intimidation or a real legal threat.
- Explain rejection of alternatives, excessive internal committees, seventeen offices, Framework unchanged.
- Offer $14.95, a cutting from the pot plant, honorary Framework certification and no director seat; don't invent this as a real offer sent to owner.
- Quote: "You may hold the domain. You do not hold the narrative."
- End with two signatures and correct titles. Tom is never co-founder.
- Small in-world satire note if necessary: "A satirical corporate statement. The only proceedings are proceedings with this bit."

Optional print action: browser print with dedicated CSS that hides header/footer/prank UI and preserves sensible page breaks. Not mandatory to create a separate static white-paper PDF unless it fits time and quality checks. It must be a standalone, substantial web page first.
